The Pomodoro focus timer accumulates focus from **real reading time**—it does not count every backlink jump on a wall clock. A full tomato counts once; a partial one can continue later. The reader shows a tomato bar and stats. Mostly premium-related; follow settings and license. Details below:

## 1. Enable and Disable
1. Open reader settings and find **Enable focus Pomodoro timer**.
2. On by default. Turning it off hides the top focus entry and the tomato bar.
3. Turn it back on when you want focus rhythm; excerpts and progress data themselves are unchanged.

> Note: Tomatoes serve “sit down and read” rhythm; they can align with tomato footprints on home stats / activity heatmap. Design: `Er-00a 设计理念下的一些考虑`.

## 2. How Time Accumulates
1. While you are reading, time fills the current tomato.
2. When full, it counts as one completed tomato (a notice may appear).
3. Leave mid-tomato: progress is kept and can continue next time—no forced reset to zero.
4. Bookshelf activity heatmaps may also show tomato activity by day (premium / trial per UI).

## 3. Display Mode
**Focus tomato display** in settings:

1. **Expanded (row of tomatoes)**: completed tomatoes in a row—easy to count.
2. **Summary (tomato + number)**: one tomato plus a count—more compact.

## 4. Bar Position
1. Default is bottom-left of the reader, near page controls.
2. Long-press the bar to drag it so it does not block body text or controls.
3. Double-click the bar, or use **Reset tomato timer position** in settings, to restore the default.
4. Reset is available only after you have dragged it at least once.

## 5. vs Remaining Reading Time
Tomatoes answer “how long did I focus this session?” Remaining reading time answers “about how long left in the chapter / book?” Both can be on. See `Er-03g 剩余阅读时间`.
